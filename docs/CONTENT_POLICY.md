# Content Policy

Open Prompt Archive is a dataset-curation project, not a general-purpose mirror of everything that can be found online.

## Core rule

Content must satisfy both:

1. the repository's licensing/provenance requirements; and
2. the repository's content-quality and distribution rules.

An open license does not guarantee inclusion.

## Accepted content

The main dataset may include prompt text and metadata from approved sources when:

- redistribution rights are verified;
- provenance is sufficiently documented;
- required attribution can be preserved;
- the record can be represented within the canonical data model;
- no stronger repository policy requires exclusion.

## Excluded by default

The main archive excludes:

- private, leaked, stolen, credential-gated, or access-controlled material;
- prompt corpora with unknown or unresolved redistribution rights;
- content copied from a third party without credible authority to license it;
- secrets, API keys, authentication tokens, passwords, private keys, session cookies, or other credentials;
- unnecessary personal data;
- content whose primary purpose is spam, malware distribution, credential theft, or abuse of the repository itself;
- Carnaverone Studio first-party/proprietary prompt collections.

## Personal data

Prompt text may contain names, handles, email addresses, URLs, or other personal information.

The archive should minimize unnecessary personal data and should not preserve private or sensitive personal information merely because it appeared in an upstream prompt collection.

Public attribution data may be retained when required for provenance or licensing, subject to review.

## Real people, brands, and copyrighted subjects

A prompt may refer to a real person, brand, fictional character, work, or trademark while the prompt text itself is openly licensed. That does not imply that every downstream generation or use is legally unrestricted.

The archive records the prompt and its provenance; it does not grant rights in third-party names, likenesses, trademarks, characters, or referenced works.

## Associated media

Images, video, audio, and other media require separate rights verification.

By default:

- prompt text may be archived after source approval;
- external media URLs may be recorded when useful and lawful;
- third-party media bytes are not mirrored automatically.

## Quality and duplication

Low-quality, malformed, or duplicate records may be corrected, merged, or excluded when the action does not destroy provenance.

Duplicate prompt text from different verified sources must preserve each source relationship.

## Model compatibility claims

Model names supplied by upstream sources may be stored as metadata, but the archive must not claim verified compatibility unless that claim is actually tested or otherwise evidenced.

## Policy enforcement

Maintainers may remove or quarantine content when new evidence reveals a licensing, provenance, privacy, security, or content-policy concern.
